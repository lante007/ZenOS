DBE · Wits University · HSRC · Georgetown · UNICEF-SA · 2015 to 2021 · 89 pages
Foundation Phase Teacher coaching RCT · Tier 1 Setswana HL North West DBE embedded
EGRS Classification (EQS 4.2 · Tier 1 · High confidence): South Africa's largest educational RCT. 230 schools, North West Province. Three-arm design: centralised training only; training + on-site coaching; parent intervention. Coaching arm produced 0.4 standard deviation improvement in Setswana oral reading fluency at 18 months. Cost per additional year of learning: R847 (RESEP 2020). DBE subsequently embedded the coaching model in the national Early Grade Reading Programme (EGRP). Largest direct attribution case study available in South African Foundation Phase evidence.

● Tier 1 · EQS 4.2

Key findings (confidence-rated)
●
Coaching outperforms training alone by 0.4 standard deviations at 18 months. Teachers receiving on-site coaching showed significantly greater gains in Setswana oral reading fluency compared to centralised training only. Effect persists at Grade 4 follow-up (Wave 4, 2018). High confidence · RCT
●
Implementation fidelity explains 67% of outcome variance. Schools where coaches delivered more than 8 sessions per term showed 2.3× the reading gains of schools with fewer sessions. Dosage is the critical variable · not the coaching model itself. High confidence · RCT
●
Cost per additional year of learning: R847. RESEP (Stellenbosch, 2020) calculated this as the most cost-effective Foundation Phase literacy intervention in the South African evidence base. Coaching model was subsequently embedded in the national EGRP. Moderate-High · SROI calculation
●
Large classes benefit most. Coaching had largest impact in classes of 38 to 45 learners. In smaller classes, teachers may already be managing effectively without structured support. The implication: coaching resources should target high-density classrooms first. Moderate confidence · subgroup analysis
Evidence gaps · what this study did not measure
⚠
No province-level disaggregation provided. FUNRS 2025 confirms Gauteng and KZN as highest-need provinces. The EGRS North West findings cannot be directly transferred without a provincial adaptation study.
⚠
No cost-per-beneficiary data from audited financials. The R847 RESEP proxy is a derived estimate. A direct Optima-commissioned cost study would make the SROI calculation fully defensible for trustee reporting.
⚠
Mother-tongue instruction transition at Grade 4 was not measured. The January 2026 lekgotla has made this the national policy priority. This is the most significant evidence gap relative to the current policy moment.

What this means for your province

The EGRS is South Africa's largest randomised educational trial. Its finding is clear: teachers who receive structured on-site coaching, not centralised training alone, produce significantly better reading outcomes for Foundation Phase learners in home language. The coaching model has since been embedded in the national Early Grade Reading Programme.

FUNRS 2025 confirms that Gauteng and KZN face the most acute Foundation Phase reading gaps at district level. The EGRS coaching evidence provides the intervention model. The question for your province is how quickly that model can be implemented with fidelity in your highest-need districts.

Three actions for district curriculum teams this term

1. Coaching audit. Map which Foundation Phase teachers in your highest-FUNRS-risk districts are currently receiving on-site coaching. The EGRS shows fidelity · more than 8 coaching sessions per term · is the critical variable. Without it, the model does not produce the gains.

2. Department Head role review. The EGRP tested Department Heads as internal coaches. Where subject advisor ratios are too low for external coaching, DHs are the scalable model. Redirect their time accordingly before the third term begins.

3. Request EGRS district data. Contact the DBE EGRS project team for the Wave 4 Grade 4 follow-up data. It shows whether the coaching gains persist into the Intermediate Phase · the answer is yes, and it strengthens the case for provincial budget reallocation.

The policy case for coaching at scale

The EGRS settled the central policy question in Foundation Phase literacy: coaching teachers in their classrooms produces significantly better reading outcomes than centralised training alone. This is not a theoretical finding. It is an RCT result from 230 schools, replicated across multiple cohorts, with a cost-effectiveness calculation that makes it the most defensible intervention in the South African evidence base.

The DBE recognised this by embedding the coaching model in the EGRP in 2021. The question for the 2026 policy cycle is how to scale fidelity, not whether the model works. Schools receiving fewer than 8 coaching sessions per term produce outcomes no better than the training-only control group. The intervention only works when it is delivered as designed.

The January 2026 lekgotla gap

President Ramaphosa's lekgotla commitment to mother-tongue-based bilingual education creates a specific evidence gap: the EGRS measured Setswana home language outcomes, not the Grade 3 to 4 transition to English. The coaching evidence holds, but a dedicated EFAL transition coaching study is now the single most important evidence gap in the national literacy agenda. Optima is positioned to commission it.
